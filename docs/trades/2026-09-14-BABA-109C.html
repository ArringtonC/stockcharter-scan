BABA $109C 10/2 — what to do differently

Bought 14 September at $4.25. Cut 16 September when it closed under $107.72. Two days later the same contract is worth $6.45. The exit was not bad luck — the stop was set closer to the entry than a normal day's move, and the entry passed no setup. Both are fixable, and one of them was my error.

Five checkpoints, in order

Each one is a place the trade could have changed. The first is the one that mattered most.

1

Does a setup fire?

What happened

BABA was 43% below its high, which is inside the Setup F band. But the 10 EMA was under the 30 and the 50 was under the 200. Both trend conditions failed. The trade was taken anyway.

What the data says

That combination is the all-trends-down bucket. It reaches its target 39% of the time against 71% when the averages are stacked up.

No entry filter fixes it. RSI at the buy point moves the win rate from 53% to 55% across every band.

The rule: if no setup fires, the trade is cash. Two of the three real trades this month passed no setup, and both are the ones that hurt.

2

How long does the thesis need?

What happened

18 days to expiry. The thesis was a recovery in a stock that had been falling for a week.

What the data says

Under 30 days, a recovery setup performs worse than a random day — 21 DTE is −14pp against random, 45 DTE is +33pp, 90 DTE is +51pp.

A 28-day at-the-money call has a −57.6% median and 46% go to zero.

The honest complication: a 60-day version of this exact trade cost $9.06 and would be worth $11.05 now — +22% against the 18-day's +52%. The short one paid better this time, because it was cheaper. It also had roughly a one-in-two chance of expiring worthless. It happened to land on the right side.

3

Where does the stop go?

What happened

Cut on a close under $107.72, the 20-day low. It fired on day three.

What the data says

Six independent tests in this project say tight stops make recovery trades strictly worse, and wider always beats tighter.

A 5% stop at $103.77 was never touched. Neither was 10%, 15% or 20%.

The stop was nearer than a coin-flip day. It was not a stop, it was a fee. I set it by anchoring to the 20-day low without checking how far away the 20-day low actually was.

4

What takes you out on the way up?

What happened

Sell at $116, the 30 EMA, or at +100%. Neither was reached before the stop fired.

What the data says

On 2,168 trades of this shape: selling into an RSI of 60 triples the median — +3.10% against +1.11% for holding — but it only fires 13% of the time.

The 30 EMA exit fires on 87% and wins 66%, but caps every winner so hard the mean falls to +0.23%.

The exit was the right shape. The 30 EMA is a profit-taking level on a position you already hold, which is exactly how it was used. It never got the chance.

5

Which clock are you reading?

What happened

The breakout looked live on a 15-minute chart with RSI at 84.6.

What the data says

The daily RSI that same moment was 47.6 — neutral, not overbought.

84.6 on a 15-minute chart is about four hours of buying. The trade had 14 days on it.

The rule: read the indicator on the clock the trade runs on. A 14-day option is a daily-chart instrument.

The workflow — run this before the next one

Five questions in order. Any red answer stops the trade there.

stop

Does a setup fire — all of its conditions, not most?

No: the trade is cash. Nine days in ten this is the answer. Setup F needs the drawdown band, 10>30, 50>200 and revenue +25%. Three of four is not a signal.

stop

Is the expiry at least 45 days out?

No: do not buy it. The only exception is VIX at 25 or above, and then at $200, not $600. Target 90 to 120 days.

size

What is the stock's median daily move?

Any stop must be at least 4× that number, or there is no stop at all and the position is sized to survive instead. BABA's 1.34% day needed a stop 5%+ away, not 1.38%.

size

How much of the premium is time value?

Over 50% means you are buying air. Under 25% means most of what you pay is real. Never over 8% of the stock price in premium — that was the single worst factor measured, at −33.8pp.

go

Write the exit before you buy.

One target, one invalidation level, one date. If you cannot write all three in a sentence, the trade is not formed yet.

The 45-day rule cost money here, and it was still right. Every longer-dated contract made less, because BABA moved 3.5% in four days and short options capture a fast move best. That is what the 45-day rule gives up. What it buys is the other outcome: had BABA kept falling, the 2 October contracts expire worthless and the December one is still alive. The rule is a probability, not a promise, and this was one of the times it paid to break it.

The order of the lessons matters. The stop being four times too tight cost $290 on this one trade, and it is the easiest thing to fix — it is arithmetic, not judgment. But the trade that never should have been taken is the one that repeats. BABA hit 39%-odds by construction, and the only reason this post-mortem has a happy counterfactual is that the coin landed the right way.

The workflow above exists so the next one is decided at checkpoint one, where it is free, instead of at checkpoint three, where it costs.
