The scanner is audited. The page was not.

Every number on Ledger is checked against a null model before it ships. The markup around those numbers had never been looked at. This is one pass over the seven tabs and the plan — what was on screen, and what is there now.

Six findings. Three were bugs that shipped and none of them showed up in a syntax check, a word count, or a tag-balance test. They were found by opening the page and looking at it.

A class name collided

Reportbug

.real is the small red REAL badge. The Report marked real-money rows with <tr class="real">, which matched the same rule — so the whole row was painted solid var(--dn) instead of the intended 5% tint. Grey text and green text on a salmon block.

Every tab said Ledger twice

all 7 tabsreadability

Each renderer came from a standalone proposal page, so each carried its own masthead. Inside the app that sits directly under the site masthead, which already says Ledger and already highlights the tab. 24 duplicate mastheads removed. The date was printed three times in two lines.

A verb that never agreed

Todaybug

The verdict is the largest type on the site and the first thing read. It had been wrong on every day more than one setup fired.

Right-aligned columns ran into the next one

Planbug

td.r zeroed padding-right. That is correct for a last column and wrong in the middle — the text had nothing between it and the next cell.

The plan explained itself four times

Planreadability

The floor line had its own section, then reappeared under three more. The checkpoint table printed the same sentence on all four rows — that is a column header, not a row. Nothing was dropped: the LEAPS correction went from three paragraphs to one line, the failure branches from prose to a table.

The most-used colour was the only one failing

every pagebug

--dim carries every label, caption and timestamp on the site, at 10.5px. It was the weakest token in both themes and the only one under WCAG AA. Tuned against --panel, the harder of the two surfaces it sits on. --amber in light was 4.2:1 across 44 elements.

Measured on the live site, 21 September 2026. Contrast is computed against the composited background — every translucent layer walked and flattened — not against the page colour, because a badge sits on its own fill. Two earlier readings in this pass were wrong for exactly that reason and are not in this list.
