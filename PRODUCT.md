# Ledger — product context

## Register
product

## Users

**Arrington, the only daily user.** Trades a real ~$8,500 Schwab account. Opens this
page once each morning before the 8:30 Central open and sometimes again near the close.
He is not a quant. He reads charts well — better than the filters, repeatedly — and he
has one documented weakness: size and structure, not entry selection. His 2017 record
showed good entries ruined by oversized short-dated options.

**The occasional visitor.** A friend he sends the link to. Knows markets, has never seen
this page, will spend ninety seconds on it. Will judge the whole project on whether the
first screen makes sense. Currently it does not.

## Product purpose

Tell one person, in under a minute, whether there is a trade today — and when there is
not, make that a satisfying answer rather than an empty one. **Nine days in ten, nothing
fires.** The page succeeds when "nothing today" reads as the system working, not
broken. That is the hardest thing it has to do and the current design does not do it.

A second job: keep him honest. Every rule on the page carries the test that produced it.
The page is a record of what was actually measured, including the things that failed.

## Tone

Plain, measured, unexcited. The voice of a research note, not a trading app. It says
"does not fire" rather than "no signals found!" It publishes its own negative results.
It never uses urgency, streaks, or encouragement to trade.

Numbers are stated with their sample size and their caveat. "83% reach the target" always
travels with "198 signals, survivorship caveat." A number without provenance is a lie by
omission here.

## Anti-references

- **Robinhood / any broker app.** Confetti, streaks, green-red dopamine, nudges to act.
  This page exists partly to counteract that.
- **TradingView dashboards.** Density as a substitute for judgment. Twelve indicators,
  no opinion.
- **Finance SaaS marketing pages.** Hero metric, gradient, "Track your portfolio like a
  pro." This is a private instrument, not a product being sold.
- **Bloomberg terminal cosplay.** Amber-on-black for the aesthetic rather than the use.

## Strategic principles

1. **The answer comes before the evidence.** VIX regime and "does anything fire" are the
   first screen. Everything else is a drill-down.
2. **Say what failed.** Setup D is labelled "watchlist only, it never beat random
   picking." The Sectors tab is labelled "context, not a signal." Removing those labels
   would make the page prettier and worse.
3. **Real money is never confused with paper money.** One red tag, unmistakable.
4. **A quiet day should look deliberate.** An empty scan is the most common state and
   should be the most composed screen, not the emptiest.
5. **Nothing on this page should make him want to trade.** Information, never
   invitation.
